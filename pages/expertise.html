Core Research Domains

Substantive expertise

Care AI

Technology that cares — or claims to

I study how "care" is operationalized in AI design: the assumptions it encodes about who receives care, who provides it, and what good care looks like. My work focuses on AI systems in nonprofit, social service, and community contexts — where the stakes of misrepresentation are highest.

Industry relevance: AI product teams building in healthcare, social services, elder care, mental health, and community technology.

Responsible AI

Beyond principles — to practice

Responsible AI principles proliferate; implementation lags. My research examines why the gap persists: how organizational incentives, technical architectures, and governance structures interact to make accountability difficult in practice. I'm developing evaluation frameworks grounded in community needs rather than corporate risk management.

Industry relevance: AI governance teams, ethics boards, policy research, and product teams building accountable systems.

Agentic AI

Autonomous action, distributed consequence

As AI systems take multi-step autonomous actions — sending communications, making recommendations, managing workflows — accountability becomes distributed and diffuse. I study how affected communities understand, experience, and contest the actions of agentic systems deployed on their behalf or about them.

Industry relevance: Teams building agentic products in enterprise, nonprofit, and public-sector contexts.

Human-Computer Interaction

Participatory methods, community-centered design

I conduct qualitative research (interviews, focus groups, ethnographic observation) and participatory design (co-design workshops, prototype co-evaluation) with communities experiencing technology harms and gaps. My methods are grounded in feminist HCI and postcolonial computing frameworks.

Industry relevance: UX research, user research, design research, and community engagement roles.
